Frequently Asked Questions About The Eiffel Tower

Why was the Eiffel Tower originally built?

It was built as the entrance arch for the 1889 World's Fair in Paris, commemorating the 100th anniversary of the French Revolution. It was intended as a temporary structure for the event.

How tall is the Eiffel Tower?

The Eiffel Tower is 330 meters (1,083 feet) tall, including the antenna. Without the antenna, it stands at 300 meters (984 feet). It was the world's tallest man-made structure for 41 years.

What material is the Eiffel Tower primarily made from?

The Eiffel Tower is primarily made from puddled iron (wrought iron). This robust material was carefully assembled using over 18,000 individual iron pieces and 2.5 million rivets, demonstrating advanced engineering for its time.

Where is the Eiffel Tower located in Paris?

The Eiffel Tower is located on the Champ de Mars, a large public greenspace, in the 7th arrondissement of Paris, France. It stands prominently near the Seine River.

Can visitors go to the very top of the Eiffel Tower?

Yes, visitors can access the top floor, which is the third level. There's an observation deck offering panoramic views of Paris. Lifts and stairs provide access to different levels, depending on the chosen ticket.

How many light bulbs illuminate the Eiffel Tower at night?

Approximately 20,000 light bulbs are used for the sparkling effect that occurs for five minutes every hour on the hour after sunset. Additionally, other lights illuminate its structure steadily throughout the night.

What is a common nickname for the Eiffel Tower?

A common nickname for the Eiffel Tower is "La Dame de Fer," which translates to "The Iron Lady." This name reflects its robust construction from puddled iron and its graceful yet strong presence.

What year was the Eiffel Tower completed?

The Eiffel Tower was completed in 1889. It was inaugurated on March 31, 1889, for the World's Fair, marking the centennial of the French Revolution and showcasing French industrial prowess.

Why was the Eiffel Tower almost demolished after the World's Fair?

It was intended to be a temporary structure, scheduled for demolition after 20 years. However, its value as a radio antenna for telegraphy and scientific experiments saved it from being dismantled.

How many steps are there to climb to the top of the Eiffel Tower?

There are 1,665 steps from the ground to the small platform at the very top. However, visitors can only climb the stairs to the second floor; the rest requires a lift.
