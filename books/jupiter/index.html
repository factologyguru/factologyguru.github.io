FACT #2

A Failed Star Prototype

Astronomers often refer to Jupiter as a "failed star" because it is made of almost the exact same ingredients as the Sun: hydrogen and helium. If Jupiter had grown about 80 times more massive during its formation, the pressure at its core would have triggered nuclear fusion, the process of smashing atoms together to create light and heat. It would have ignited into a second sun, turning our solar system into a binary star system like the ones we see in science fiction movies. Instead, it stayed just small enough to remain a planet, forever trapped in the "in-between" stage of cosmic evolution, glowing dimly with its own internal heat but never quite catching fire.

FACT #8

The Strongest Magnetic Field

If you could see Jupiter's magnetosphere from Earth with the naked eye, it would look several times larger than the full moon in our sky. Jupiter's magnetic field is 14 times stronger than Earth's, largely due to that swirling sea of metallic hydrogen in its core. This field creates a massive "bubble" of protection around the planet, but it also traps high-energy particles, creating a radiation belt so intense that it would kill an unprotected human in seconds. NASA has to wrap the "brain" of its Juno spacecraft in a titanium vault just to keep the radiation from frying the computer circuits as it flies past.

FACT #15

Suicidal Mission of Galileo

In 2003, NASA intentionally crashed the Galileo spacecraft into Jupiter's atmosphere. This wasn't a mistake; it was a deliberate move to protect the environment. Galileo had been orbiting Jupiter for eight years and was running out of fuel. Scientists feared that if the craft were left to drift, it might eventually crash into the moon Europa and contaminate its hidden ocean with Earth bacteria. To prevent this, they steered Galileo into the crushing depths of Jupiter, where it was vaporized in seconds. It was a noble sacrifice for a robot that had completely rewritten our understanding of the Jovian system.

FACT #16

Juno's Polar Discovery

When the Juno spacecraft arrived at Jupiter in 2016, it took the first-ever photos of the planet's north and south poles. Scientists expected to see stripes like the rest of the planet, but instead, they found a chaotic mess of giant, Earth-sized cyclones. At the North Pole, there is one central cyclone surrounded by eight others, all dancing in a stable geometric pattern. These polar storms are a deep, eerie blue and behave unlike anything seen at the equator. This discovery proved that Jupiter's atmosphere is far more complex than a simple "layer cake" of clouds, with different physics dominating at the top and bottom.

FACT #19

Jupiter's Faint Rings

Most people think of Saturn when they hear about rings, but Jupiter has them too. They are just much thinner and darker, made mostly of dust kicked off by small moons when they are hit by meteors. These rings were only discovered in 1979 when the Voyager 1 spacecraft flew past and looked back at the planet toward the Sun. Because the dust particles are so small, they are only visible when they are backlit by sunlight, much like dust motes dancing in a sunbeam in your living room. They are divided into three main sections: the halo, the main ring, and the gossamer rings.

Frequently Asked Questions About Jupiter

What is Jupiter known for being?

Jupiter is known for being the largest planet in our solar system, with a mass more than twice that of all other planets combined. It is a gas giant.

What is the Great Red Spot?

The Great Red Spot is a persistent, high-pressure storm on Jupiter, larger than Earth, that has been observed for centuries. It rotates counter-clockwise.

What is Jupiter primarily made of?

Jupiter is primarily made of hydrogen and helium, like a small star. It lacks a solid surface, consisting mostly of dense fluid layers around a probable rocky core.

How many moons does Jupiter have?

Jupiter has 95 confirmed moons. Its four largest, the Galilean moons (Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto), were discovered by Galileo Galilei and are visible with binoculars.

Why does Jupiter have such a strong magnetic field?

Jupiter has an incredibly strong magnetic field, generated by the rapid rotation of its metallic hydrogen interior. This field creates powerful aurorae at its poles.

How fast does Jupiter rotate?

Jupiter rotates extremely fast, completing a full rotation in just under 10 hours. This rapid rotation contributes to its flattened shape and powerful atmospheric jet streams.

Can Jupiter be seen with rings?

Yes, Jupiter has a faint ring system, though it is much less prominent than Saturn's. These rings are composed of dust particles kicked up from its small inner moons by meteoroid impacts.

Why is Jupiter sometimes called a "failed star"?

Jupiter is sometimes called a "failed star" because its composition is similar to the Sun (mostly hydrogen and helium). However, it lacks sufficient mass to ignite nuclear fusion in its core.

What makes Europa an interesting moon?

Europa is an interesting moon because it is believed to harbor a vast subsurface ocean of liquid water beneath its icy crust. This ocean is considered a prime location for potential extraterrestrial life.

How does Jupiter influence our solar system?

Jupiter influences our solar system significantly with its immense gravity. It helps clear away comets and asteroids, protecting inner planets, but can also perturb their orbits.
