Frequently Asked Questions About The Mosquito

Why is the mosquito considered the deadliest animal on Earth?

Mosquitoes kill more humans than any other animal by transmitting diseases like malaria, dengue, and yellow fever. Their bites spread pathogens responsible for over 700,000 deaths every year.

How do mosquitoes find humans to bite?

Mosquitoes detect carbon dioxide from breath, body heat, and skin odor from up to 50 meters away. Females rely on these cues to track down a blood source for their eggs.

Why do only female mosquitoes bite?

Female mosquitoes need protein from blood to develop their eggs, while males feed only on nectar and plant juices. Males also lack the specialized mouthparts needed to pierce skin.

How long do mosquitoes typically live?

Most adult mosquitoes live two to four weeks, though some species can survive several months in cooler conditions. Males generally live shorter lives than females.

What diseases do mosquitoes transmit?

Mosquitoes spread malaria, dengue fever, Zika virus, West Nile virus, and yellow fever, among dozens of other illnesses. Different species tend to specialize in transmitting different pathogens.

How fast can a mosquito fly?

Mosquitoes are surprisingly slow fliers, reaching speeds of only about 1 to 1.5 miles per hour. Their erratic, zigzagging flight pattern is what makes them so hard to swat.

Why do mosquito bites itch?

When a mosquito bites, it injects saliva containing anticoagulants to keep blood flowing freely. The human immune system reacts to proteins in that saliva, producing the itchy, swollen bump.

Do mosquitoes serve any ecological purpose?

Mosquito larvae are an important food source for fish and other aquatic species, and adults pollinate certain plants. Despite their reputation, they play a real role in several food webs.

How many species of mosquito exist?

Scientists have identified more than 3,500 mosquito species worldwide, found on every continent except Antarctica. Only a small fraction of these species regularly bite humans.

Why do mosquitoes need standing water to breed?

Female mosquitoes lay their eggs in still water, where larvae develop before emerging as adults. Even small amounts, like a bottle cap of rainwater, can support a breeding cycle.
