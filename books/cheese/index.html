Frequently Asked Questions About Cheese

What is cheese made from?

Cheese is primarily made from milk, typically from cows, goats, or sheep. It involves curdling the milk, pressing the curds, and often aging them.

How many types of cheese exist?

There are thousands of different types of cheese worldwide, varying by milk source, region, aging process, and added cultures, creating unique flavors and textures.

Why does some cheese have holes?

Cheese like Swiss has holes, called 'eyes,' due to gas produced by specific bacteria during the ripening process. These gases create bubbles that form the characteristic holes.

Can cheese be healthy?

Yes, cheese can be a good source of calcium, protein, and vitamins. However, it can also be high in fat and sodium, so moderation is key.

Where is cheese believed to have originated?

Cheese making is believed to have originated in the Middle East or Central Asia, with archaeological evidence suggesting its practice as early as 8,000 years ago.

What is the role of rennet in cheesemaking?

Rennet is an enzyme that causes milk to coagulate, separating into solid curds and liquid whey. This is a crucial step for forming the cheese mass.

How should cheese be stored to maintain freshness?

Cheese should be stored in the refrigerator, ideally wrapped in cheese paper or parchment paper. This allows it to breathe while preventing it from drying out.

Why do some cheeses have mold on their rinds?

Some cheeses, like Brie or Blue cheese, have edible mold on their rinds because specific molds are intentionally introduced to develop unique flavors and textures during aging.

Can all types of milk be used to make cheese?

Yes, various types of milk, including cow, goat, sheep, buffalo, and even yak milk, can be used to produce a wide array of cheeses with distinct characteristics.

What is an aged cheese?

An aged cheese is one that has been ripened for an extended period, allowing complex flavors to develop and textures to firm up as moisture evaporates over time.
