Frequently Asked Questions About Big Ben

What is Big Ben?

Big Ben is the nickname for the Great Bell inside the Elizabeth Tower, part of the Houses of Parliament in London. It is one of the UK's most iconic landmarks.

Where is Big Ben located?

Big Ben is located at the northern end of the Palace of Westminster in London, United Kingdom, overlooking the River Thames.

Why is it called Big Ben?

It is called Big Ben after the largest bell within the tower, weighing over 13 tons. The name is often used to refer to the entire clock tower.

How tall is the Elizabeth Tower?

The Elizabeth Tower, which houses Big Ben, stands approximately 96 meters (316 feet) tall from ground level to the tip of its spire.

Can visitors go inside Big Ben?

No, generally public access to the Elizabeth Tower and Big Ben is restricted. Tours were previously available only to UK residents by special arrangement, but are currently suspended.

What is the official name of the tower?

The official name of the tower housing Big Ben is the Elizabeth Tower. It was renamed in 2012 from the Clock Tower to celebrate Queen Elizabeth II's Diamond Jubilee.

How accurate is the Big Ben clock?

The Big Ben clock is famously accurate. Its mechanism is meticulously maintained, and its time is occasionally adjusted by adding or removing old penny coins to the pendulum.

What is the history of Big Ben's first ringing?

Big Ben first chimed on May 31, 1859, marking its initial operation. The Great Clock officially began keeping time on September 7, 1859, becoming an iconic London sound.

Why did Big Ben undergo a major conservation project?

Big Ben underwent a major conservation project from 2017 to 2022 to repair critical structural issues, restore its intricate stonework, and upgrade its clock mechanism for future generations.

What sound does Big Ben make?

Big Ben makes a distinctive "bong" sound for the hours, with smaller bells chiming the quarter-hours. Its iconic chimes are broadcast daily on BBC Radio.
