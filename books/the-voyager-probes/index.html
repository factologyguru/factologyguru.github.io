Frequently Asked Questions About The Voyager Probes

What are the Voyager Probes?

The Voyager Probes are two robotic spacecraft, Voyager 1 and Voyager 2, launched by NASA in 1977 to study the outer Solar System and interstellar space.

How were the Voyager Probes launched?

The Voyager Probes were launched in 1977. Voyager 2 launched on August 20, and Voyager 1 launched on September 5, both aboard Titan-Centaur rockets from Cape Canaveral.

Where are the Voyager Probes now?

The Voyager Probes are currently in interstellar space, having exited the heliosphere. They are the farthest human-made objects from Earth, continuing their journey into the cosmos.

What is the primary mission of the Voyager Probes?

The primary mission was to explore Jupiter and Saturn. Voyager 2 extended this to Uranus and Neptune, completing a "grand tour" of the outer planets.

How do the Voyager Probes communicate with Earth?

The Voyager Probes communicate using their 3.7-meter high-gain antennas and NASA's Deep Space Network. Radio signals, traveling at light speed, take over 20 hours to reach Earth.

Why are the Voyager Probes still operational after so long?

The Voyager Probes are still operational due to their robust design and radioisotope thermoelectric generators (RTGs) which convert heat from plutonium decay into electricity, providing power for decades.

What is the Golden Record?

The Golden Record is a phonograph record carried by each Voyager probe. It contains sounds and images selected to portray the diversity of life and culture on Earth for any intelligent extraterrestrial life.

Can the Voyager Probes be retrieved?

No, the Voyager Probes cannot be retrieved. They are traveling at immense speeds far beyond the Solar System, making retrieval technologically impossible with current capabilities.

How fast are the Voyager Probes traveling?

Voyager 1 is traveling at approximately 17 kilometers per second (38,000 mph) relative to the Sun, and Voyager 2 slightly slower. These speeds allow them to escape the Solar System.

What scientific discoveries have the Voyager Probes made?

The Voyager Probes discovered new moons, active volcanism on Io, complex ring systems, and detailed atmospheric features of the gas giants, fundamentally changing our understanding of the outer Solar System.
