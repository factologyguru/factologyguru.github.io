Frequently Asked Questions About Wine

What is wine made from?

Wine is primarily made from fermented grape juice. Yeast converts the natural sugars in the grapes into alcohol and carbon dioxide, creating the alcoholic beverage.

How is red wine different from white wine?

Red wine is made with the skins of red or black grapes, which impart color, flavor, and tannins. White wine is typically made from the pulp of grapes, often without the skins.

Can wine go bad?

Yes, wine can go bad. Opened wine usually oxidizes and loses its freshness within a few days. Unopened wine can also spoil if stored improperly or past its optimal drinking window.

Why do people swirl wine in a glass?

Swirling wine aerates it, releasing aromatic compounds and allowing the wine's full bouquet to be appreciated. It also helps assess the wine's viscosity and clarity.

What is a sommelier?

A sommelier is a trained and knowledgeable wine professional, typically working in fine dining restaurants. They specialize in wine service, food pairings, and cellar management, guiding guests through wine selections.

Where should wine be stored?

Wine should be stored in a cool, dark place with consistent temperature and moderate humidity. Laying bottles on their side keeps corks moist, preventing oxidation. Avoid direct sunlight and vibrations.

How long can an opened bottle of wine last?

An opened bottle of wine usually lasts 3-5 days, depending on the type and storage conditions. Red wines and full-bodied whites last longer than delicate whites and sparkling wines.

Can all wines be aged for a long time?

No, not all wines benefit from long aging. Most wines are meant to be consumed within a few years of release. Only a small percentage, typically high-quality reds and some whites, are built for extended cellaring.

Why are some wines sweet and others dry?

The sweetness or dryness of wine depends on the amount of residual sugar remaining after fermentation. Dry wines have almost no residual sugar, while sweet wines retain more unfermented sugar.

What are tannins in wine?

Tannins are naturally occurring compounds found in grape skins, seeds, and stems, and in oak barrels. They contribute bitterness, astringency, and structure to wine, especially red wine, and aid in aging.
