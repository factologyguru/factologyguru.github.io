Frequently Asked Questions About The Empire State Building

What is the Empire State Building?

It is a 102-story Art Deco skyscraper in Midtown Manhattan, New York City. For nearly 40 years, it held the title of the world's tallest building, an iconic global landmark.

Where is the Empire State Building located?

It is located at 350 Fifth Avenue, between 33rd and 34th Streets, in Midtown Manhattan, New York City, a prominent landmark in the cityscape.

Why is the Empire State Building famous?

It is famous for its iconic Art Deco architecture, its status as a cultural symbol, and its breathtaking observatory views of New York City, attracting millions of visitors.

How tall is the Empire State Building?

The building itself is 1,250 feet (381 meters) to its top floor. Including its antenna, it reaches a total height of 1,454 feet (443.2 meters).

What year was the Empire State Building completed?

It was officially completed and opened on April 11, 1931, after a remarkably fast construction period of just one year and 45 days.

Can visitors go to the top of the Empire State Building?

Yes, visitors can access two observation decks: a popular outdoor deck on the 86th floor and a more exclusive, enclosed deck on the 102nd floor.

How long did it take to build the Empire State Building?

It took a remarkable one year and 45 days to construct, starting in March 1930 and completing in April 1931, a testament to rapid engineering.

Why does the Empire State Building have different colored lights?

The building's mast lights are illuminated with various colors to celebrate holidays, events, and causes throughout the year, making it a dynamic part of the skyline.

What architectural style is the Empire State Building?

It is a prime example of the Art Deco architectural style. This style is characterized by its geometric patterns, streamlined forms, and decorative elements, popular in the 1920s and 30s.

Can you see the Empire State Building from far away?

Yes, its impressive height and prominent location make it visible from many parts of New York City, New Jersey, and even Connecticut on clear days.
